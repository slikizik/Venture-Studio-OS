# VSO Product Doctrine

## Product definition

Venture Studio OS (VSO) is an **AI Product Development Operating System** for an owner who wants to create and continuously improve commercial-quality products without personally supervising routine execution all day.

VSO transforms **human intent → structured product design → executable work → verified evidence → release → learning**, while escalating only decisions that genuinely require owner judgment.

## Primary owner outcome

The owner should be able to operate like a founder/product owner rather than a full-time developer or project administrator.

The owner is responsible for:

- desired outcomes and priorities;
- taste and acceptance;
- commercial intent;
- consequential decisions;
- approving meaningful trade-offs.

VSO is responsible for supplying repeatable development discipline, coordinating agents, exposing evidence, enforcing quality gates, preserving history, recovering safely, and minimizing unnecessary owner attention.

## The two non-negotiable sides of VSO

VSO succeeds only when both sides are strong:

1. **Commercial-quality production discipline** — requirements, architecture, benchmarking, evidence, testing, review, release readiness, and continuous improvement.
2. **Governed autonomy** — queues, agents, remote control, recovery, checkpoints, safe escalation, multi-project isolation, and cross-workstation continuity.

Autonomy without quality produces mediocre output faster. Quality without autonomy fails the owner's lifestyle objective.

## Five product-development engines

### 1. Intent Engine
Captures the problem, audience, desired outcome, constraints, non-goals, success measures, commercial target, and owner priorities. It converts vague ideas into an explicit Project Intent Brief without requiring the owner to know technical architecture.

### 2. Design Engine
Transforms approved intent into requirements, acceptance criteria, deliverables, architecture or content structure, dependencies, and executable work packets. It must surface consequential trade-offs instead of silently inventing them.

### 3. Execution Engine
Coordinates human and AI agents against approved work packets, dependencies, checkpoints, evidence expectations, retry rules, and stop conditions. Hermes is an executor used by this engine, not the definition of the engine itself.

### 4. Quality Engine
Determines whether the product is merely functional or genuinely ready for its intended commercial standard. It evaluates requirements, evidence, benchmark targets, functional quality, UX, reliability, maintainability, security, accessibility, performance, documentation, installation, and project-type-specific quality dimensions.

### 5. Learning Engine
Turns bugs, feedback, benchmark gaps, test failures, owner observations, user evidence, and commercial learning into controlled change requests, backlog items, revised requirements, and future releases.

## Core operating loop

`Intent → Design → Execute → Verify → Approve/Release → Learn → Improve`

The loop must be usable continuously after the first release.

## Progressive technical disclosure

The owner must not need to understand low-level implementation details to operate VSO. VSO should present:

1. plain-language outcome and recommendation;
2. trade-offs and impact;
3. architecture/workflow view;
4. technical rationale;
5. evidence/tests;
6. raw implementation details when requested.

## Human attention principle

The system should optimize the owner's attention, not eliminate owner control. Routine decisions are automated inside approved boundaries. High-consequence, ambiguous, commercial, architectural, privacy, security, destructive, or scope-changing choices are escalated with concise options and a recommendation.

## Commercial-quality principle

“Works” is not equivalent to “commercially ready.” A release may pass functional tests and still fail a commercial quality gate. Each project must define a Quality Profile and commercial benchmark appropriate to its type.

## Universal-project principle

The operating model is generic; project templates specialize it. Software, books, learning products, business ventures, and creative products share the same core concepts of intent, deliverables, work, evidence, review, release, and learning.

## VSO dogfooding principle

After the Smallest Executable VSO Loop is proven, **VSO itself becomes the first serious project managed inside VSO**. Subsequent VSO development should progressively move into the system so the product proves its own operating model.

## Product decision priority

When requirements conflict, prefer the choice that best preserves this order:

1. correctness and data safety;
2. product intent;
3. commercial quality;
4. owner control over consequential decisions;
5. autonomous continuity and low attention cost;
6. simplicity and maintainability;
7. convenience or visual polish.
